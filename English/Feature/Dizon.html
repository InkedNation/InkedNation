The Invisible Scars

Written by Christian Kyle R. Dizon

What if the place where students are supposed to feel safe becomes the place they experience fear?

Scars are not always visible to people’s eyes; sometimes, they’re hidden behind smiles, laughs, and normal conversations. A student goes to school and hangs out with friends, yet carries fear that no one else can see. Violence turns the safest places, even schools, into places that cause fear and discomfort. Scars on skin may heal over time, but scars on their mind can linger after a long time. After all, not all scars bleed and heal immediately, and not every cry makes a sound.

The effects of school violence go far beyond the moment it happens and affect not only physically, but also mentally. For those who witness school violence or threats, fear can become a burden creeping into their lives. Students may experience anxiety, have difficulty concentrating, or feel discomfort in school. Threats or reminders of past incidents can make them more insecure, while their silence covers an ongoing struggle with fear and distress.

Exposure to school violence can change how students see themselves and the world around them. They struggle to trust others, distance themselves from social interactions, or remain alert to danger. They may hesitate to express their feelings, as fearing judgment or misunderstanding stays in their mind. These often go unnoticed, especially when they continue attending classes and interacting with others as usual, as if nothing happened. Even those who actively participate in classes or conversations may carry an emotional burden hidden from those around them.

Being silent and looking normal does not always mean they are fine and not suffering.

This shows that understanding the impact of school violence needs more than looking at what happened; it also means recognizing the struggles that students may carry afterward. Schools provide spaces where they can speak without fear and receive the support they need. Listening and acknowledging struggles are meaningful steps toward a sense of security. After all, healing is not always a process that others can witness; sometimes, it happens quietly. It may leave no mark in people’s eyes, but its effects will echo in the minds of those who have experienced it.

And perhaps the greatest act of understanding is learning to recognize that behind every seemingly ordinary student may be a story of fear and scars that the eyes cannot see.
