A Victim’s Punishment

Written by Justin Bennar DL. Marquez

“A voice should be heard, be it big or small”. For the past few months, many students have been mentally scarred by acts of school violence and threats. For schools and the local government whose main priorities should be the victims, they prioritize the abusers first before them. There should be a steady flow of mental health support for the victims of such acts that would help them to heal and process their thoughts to avoid more severe mental health problems.

Throughout the recent academic year of 2026-2027, there have been 3 major school shootings, mainly in Tacloban, Zamboanga, and Banga, South Cotabato. According to BBC News, these have led to the deaths and severe injuries of many students, as well as the deaths of the perpetrators by suicide. According to ABC News, there were also 50 uncovered planned school shootings following the shooting in Banga.

These have left many communities, families, and especially students mentally scarred due to trauma and the possible danger looming over them. Without proper support or care, these could turn into severe mental health disorders, which could negatively affect a student’s performance in their academic and social life.

Awadalla et. al. (2024) and Weidman et al. (2015) state that depressive and anxiety symptoms have a significant impact on a student’s academic performance and excellence. They showed how students who experienced severe mental health problems struggled more in their schoolwork compared to those who don’t have these types of problems.

Schools should provide better mental health care to students, especially to those who have suffered more in these incidents. Through this, students will be able to focus on their mental well-being and avoid more severe forms of mental health issues. Schools should lessen seminars and have a more interactive approach to helping students with their coping and healing. They should offer a temporary suspension of classes, as well as adapting their support to fit a student’s needs, as different people have different ways of coping.

School shootings should be prevented and be accounted for before they start, yet this shouldn’t take our focus away from the students who have experienced such and are suffering due to it. A student’s mental health shouldn’t be overlooked, as every voice matters, no matter how big or small it is.
